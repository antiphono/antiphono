01 Who this is for

You have a product in marketand no design team behind it.

You have a product in market and some funding behind you. You do not have an internal design team, and hiring one is a year of work and a salary you are not ready to commit to. What you need is senior product thinking on the thing in front of you, now.

That is who we work with, and we have been doing it since 2018.

02 What changed

What has changedabout this work

Production and synthesis used to absorb most of a design budget. Drawing every screen, writing every summary, rebuilding every option somebody wanted to compare. That cost has collapsed, and the money it freed up moves into the parts that decide whether a product works.

Then

Static mocks, handed over for somebody else to interpret. A specification to read between the lines of. Every option redrawn and re-estimated, so one safe idea shipped and everybody hoped.

Now

Working software, built from real components. Several directions compared side by side, with real behaviour deciding between them. A new option costs a prompt and a branch instead of a rebuild.

Still true

Nothing you relied on has gone. Research, strategy, design craft and judgement all still matter, and they matter more than they did.

03 The engagement

What working with usis actually like

Five things are true of every engagement.

  1. 01

    You deal with the person doing the work.

    No account layer, no junior team, no handover to somebody you have not met. The studio is small and senior on purpose.

  2. 02

    You see working software early, not pictures of it.

    Clickable prototypes built from real components, usually more than one direction at a time. The conversation becomes which of these is better, rather than whether this is right at all.

  3. 03

    Your developers stay in control of what ships.

    We build front end code and hand it over. Your team reviews, merges and deploys through your own pipeline, under your own approval rules. We do not deploy to your production environment and we do not own your release process.

  4. 04

    You own everything, including the reasoning.

    Code, designs, research, the design system and a written record of why each decision was made all live in your repositories and your accounts. If we stop working together, nothing has to be handed back, because nothing was ever held.

  5. 05

    You can see exactly where AI is used, and where it is not.

    People run the interviews and make the calls. AI compresses synthesis, production and documentation. On any piece of work we will tell you which is which, without being asked.

04 Onboarding

The first thirty days

  1. W1

    We agree the problem, not the solution.

    We go through what you already know: the analytics, the support tickets, whatever research exists. We talk to your users. You get a written problem statement you can disagree with before anybody designs anything.

  2. W2–3

    Directions you can click.

    Two or three prototype directions, built from real components rather than drawn, on a real URL. Your team walks through them. Review becomes a working session rather than a hand back.

  3. W4

    A decision, and a plan.

    One direction is chosen, and the reasons the others were rejected are written down. You get a plan for building it with a number against it, and everything produced so far is already yours.

    You can stop there. Some people do, and the problem statement and the prototypes are worth having on their own.

06 Process

How the work runs

One process, from the problem to the live result.

  1. 01

    Design

    Problem statement, research, prototype directions built from live components, one sign off gate before engineering effort is committed.

  2. 02

    Build

    Working front end code, built from the design system, with measurement wired in as it is written.

  3. 03

    Testing

    Internal review, QA and participant testing, then live variants measured against real traffic.

  4. 04

    The record

    Every stage writes what it learned as the work happens, so the next brief starts from evidence rather than memory.

Speed is only worth having if the work holds up. Every stage has a gate, a person signs off before anything moves forward, and accessibility is checked as we go rather than audited at the end.
