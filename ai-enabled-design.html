Why Antiphono, why now

Design is not becoming less human. It is becoming faster, smarter, and more responsive to real people.

Antiphony is a musical term for call and response — two voices in dialogue, each shaping the other. It's the shape of the relationship between human judgement and AI, and it's why we changed the name: design, research and development have shifted fundamentally in the last two years, and our traditional services — UX design, research, strategy, prototyping — now move at a different speed because of it. Nothing you've relied on is gone; what's changed is how fast it moves and how much of it compounds.

Why the industry is changing

The stages haven't changed. What moves between them has.

Then

Design handed over drawings for somebody else to interpret and rebuild. Static mocks, a spec to read between the lines of — every option redrawn and re-estimated, so teams tested one safe idea and hoped in production.

Now

The design stage produces working software — production components, not pictures of them. That single change is what makes everything after it affordable: an option costs a prompt and a branch, so teams compare several and let real behaviour decide.

What this means for you

Faster prototyping

Idea to something testable in days, not weeks.

Testing at scale

Real behaviour, continuously — not periodic research rounds.

Team enablement

AI workflows embedded into your own processes.

Design governance

Consistency across products without proportional headcount.

Better value

Less budget on routine tasks, more on what shapes the product.

The whole process

One process, end to end.

Most organisations run a design process and a development process that meet awkwardly in the middle. Antiphono runs one continuous pipeline instead — one design system governing everything below it, each stage filing what it learns so the record returns to the next brief.

Section 01 — Design

Decide what to build. Make it real enough to judge.

AI-enabled design doesn't mean one tool for everything — it means the right route for what you're making, every time arriving at the same real, on-brand, clickable result. A new product feature becomes a working prototype in Claude Code. A campaign page is designed in Claude. Templated social and email assets run through Canva or Adobe Express. A new pattern for the design system itself is drawn by hand in Figma. The team, the tools and the steps below are all adjustable — this is simply how it works by default.

Section 02 — Development

Get it live without holding your breath.

Because the design stage already produces real, working components, development doesn't start from a blank page — it starts from something a product owner has already clicked through and signed off. That pulls non-developers into the process much later than usual: reviewing an actual sandbox URL instead of a ticket description, approving a live preview before it ever reaches a customer. The work still moves through the same reviewed environments — a private preview, an internal link, a rehearsal, then live — there's just far more of it visible to the whole team along the way.

2.2 Folder structure

Identical on every machine, and in the repo.

A convention Claude keeps to every time, so a new feature is filed the same way whoever starts it — and the repo stays navigable for non-technical team members.

An individual project folder

Everything for one product lives in one repo, with a single file — CLAUDE.md — acting as the contract every session reads first: your environment URLs, naming conventions and guardrails, so a new team member's first build already follows your rules. Work is organised by feature, and a feature is never overwritten — a new direction ships as a new version alongside the last one, so the safe option is always still there.

<angle brackets> are placeholders you replace with your own names
<product>/ — one repo per product CLAUDE.md — env URLs, conventions, guardrails — every session reads this first knowledge/ — decisions/ research/ tests/ conventions/ features/ <feature-name>/ — one folder per feature v1/ — the original, still live v2/ — a new direction, shipped alongside it <feature-name>/ — and the next feature after that brands/ — <brand-name>/ per brand, for multi-brand output shared/ — anything two features both use

Claude creates these folders itself and keeps to the convention every time — one session per related area of work, so context stays sharp and nothing gets overwritten.

The whole organisation

Zoom out and the same idea repeats at company scale. The design system is its own repo, released as a version rather than edited in place — every product repo then consumes whichever version it's ready for, so an upstream change never breaks a live page unannounced.

Section 03 — Testing

Check it four times. Only the last one involves customers.

Because every prototype ships with its measurement already built in, testing doesn't start from scratch either — it inherits tracking that already works. Each of the four gates below produces real evidence rather than just an approval: what a reviewer says, what QA finds, what customers actually do. Every finding is captured automatically and written into the knowledge base, so a decision made in testing this month is still available as evidence for a design brief next year.

Outcome

Because versions run side by side, testing live becomes a measurement, not a risk.

Section 04 — Compounding knowledge

Most teams cannot explain their own product a year later.

Every stage of the process already produces evidence — research findings, the reasons an option was rejected, what QA found, how real customers behaved. None of it is left to evaporate: it's captured automatically as the work happens and organised into one place the whole team can query in plain language. That turns individual pieces of user and business intelligence into a compounding asset — this year's testing becomes next year's starting evidence, not a forgotten conversation.

Outcome

A record you can ask questions of: why this way, what was the evidence, did it work?
