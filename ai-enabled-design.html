Why Antiphono, why now

“AI is data, and data can only look backwards. Creativity looks forwards.”

Design is not becoming less human. It is becoming faster, smarter, and more responsive to real people.

What "Antiphono" means

Antiphony is a musical term for call and response — two voices in dialogue, each shaping the other.

It's the shape of the relationship between human judgement and AI, and it's why we changed the name: design, research and development have shifted fundamentally in the last two years, and our traditional services — UX design, research, strategy, prototyping — now move at a different speed because of it. Nothing you've relied on is gone; what's changed is how fast it moves and how much of it compounds.

Why the industry is changing

AI cuts the cost of production and synthesis. That budget moves into strategy — and output goes up.

Then

Static mocks and drawings, handed over for somebody else to interpret.

A spec to read between the lines of, not something to run.

Every option redrawn and re-estimated — one safe idea, tested by hoping in production.

Now

Working software — production components, not pictures of them.

Several directions compared side by side, and real behaviour decides.

A new option costs a prompt and a branch, not a rebuild.

What this means for you

Faster prototyping

Idea to something testable in days, not weeks.

Testing at scale

Real behaviour, continuously — not periodic research rounds.

Team enablement

AI workflows embedded into your own processes.

Design governance

Consistency across products without proportional headcount.

Better value

Less budget on routine tasks, more on what shapes the product.

The whole process

One process, end to end.

Most organisations run a design process and a development process that meet awkwardly in the middle. Antiphono runs one continuous pipeline instead — one design system governing everything below it, each stage filing what it learns so the record returns to the next brief.

Section 03 — Testing

Product testing is no longer cost and time prohibitive

Combining a dramatic drop in production time with far more automated analysis means testing can be far more robust — both internally and with real users.
