Why companies work with us

Six reasons, in the order they usually matter

( 01 )

You get a design team without hiring one

Senior product design, research and front end capability, available when you need it and not on your payroll. No recruitment, no ramp up, no salary commitment before you know what the work is worth.

( 02 )

We have already worked out the AI part

Most teams know AI should be changing how they design and build, and have not had time to work out how. We have spent two years on it. You get the method, the guardrails and the tooling decisions without doing that work yourself, and if you want your own team running it, we hand it over.

( 03 )

You see working prototypes in days

Real, clickable software built from real components, usually more than one direction at a time. That means you can put something in front of your team on Tuesday and in front of users the following week, then do it again. Feedback stops being an event you schedule and becomes something you can repeat.

( 04 )

Your budget covers more

Production and synthesis used to absorb most of a design budget. They no longer do. The same money now buys more research, more directions tested and more evidence behind what ships. Our rates have not dropped. What you get for them has changed.

( 05 )

Your developers stay in control

We are not a replacement for your engineering team and we do not bid against one. We build front end code and components, then hand them over. Your team reviews, merges and ships through your own pipeline. We do not deploy to your production environment and we do not own your release process.

( 06 )

You own everything, including the reasoning

Code, designs, research, the design system, and a written record of why every decision was made. All of it in your repositories and your accounts. Nothing is held back to keep you dependent, and if we stop working together there is nothing to hand over because it was always yours.

Why this is possible now

You are limited by how well you decide.

Making things used to be the expensive part. Drawing every screen. Writing every summary. Rebuilding every option somebody wanted to compare.

That cost has mostly gone. Judgement has not. So the constraint moved. You are no longer limited by how much you can produce. Most teams are still working around a constraint that disappeared, which is why the design process at a lot of companies still looks like it did in 2019.

How we work

One process, from the problem to the live result

Most organisations run a design process and a development process that meet awkwardly in the middle. The gap between them is where the money goes. Antiphono runs one process instead.

Design

A problem to solve, not a solution to build. We research it, then build several directions from live components rather than drawing them. One sign off before engineering effort is committed.

Build

Working front end code, with measurement wired in as it is written. Built on a branch, following your conventions.

Test

Internal review on a real URL, then QA and participant testing, then variants running against real traffic. Producing a variant is cheap now, so testing stops being the first thing cut when the timeline tightens.

Record

Every stage writes down what it learned, in your repository, next to the code it describes. What was decided, what was rejected, what the behaviour showed. Month twelve costs less and delivers more than month one, and that is the reason relationships here run for years.

Someone reviews and signs off before anything moves forward. Moving quickly does not cost you control.
